Eos and Helios

The Eosian-Helion System is a binary star system with 17 bodies in p-type orbits around the two stars. Eos and Helios are the two G-type main-sequence stars that occupy the center of the star system, and are nicknamed “Twin Sisters” for their similarities to each other as sister stars. The system is unique with multiple planets “sharing” the same orbit and consisting of a single known habitable planet - Kalypso.

Conambit Orbits: The Eosian-Helion System consists of 5 main conambit orbits within its star system, mainly planets past the Trojan Belt and in the Horae Orbit. Conambit orbit, a term coined in 1523 by astronomer Darya Sakovich, is an orbit in which two celestial bodies are far away enough not to be affected by their Pavlic radius, but close enough for their orbits to be observed as a singular “orbit zone”.

Currently, the system has 17 observable and labeled celestial bodies, alongside hundreds of moons, listed in the order from perihelion to aphelion:

Locality Zone

  1. Epaphus
  2. Chrison
  3. Kalypso
  4. Eris
Trojan Belt
  1. Hekate, Minos, Zarex
  2. Itonus, Leos
  3. Dias, Myrina
Horae Orbit
  1. Auxo, Thallo, Carpo
  2. Telamon, Geogrios, Daimon
